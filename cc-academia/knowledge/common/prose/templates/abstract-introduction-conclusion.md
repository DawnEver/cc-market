# Abstract, introduction and conclusion: common moves

Source: common. Rules cited by id; a field's `prose/templates/moves.md` refines the moves, an author's
templates fix the layer budget. The author leads the logic of these parts (dimension process.author-led-parts; skills/write hard constraints).

## Abstract (structure.funnel-opening, abstract.gap-before-results, structure.funnel-opening)

1. Broad shared context (trend and its demand on the object).
2. Narrowing, one level per sentence: application, object class, regime, problem.
3. Gap: what current practice leaves open, stated as what prior work covered (argument.prior-work-as-found).
4. What this paper does: object, model, method; specialty concepts by role before symbol.
5. Findings, each a mechanism or direction; values only as dimension numbers.abstract-conclusion allows.
6. Broad relevance: the implication for the application.

## Introduction (argument.background-serves, cohesion.hook-previous, introduction.roadmap)

1. Broad context, narrowing to the object and its problem mechanism.
2. Specialty concepts named with their classic reference and the one property the argument needs
   (audience.general-practitioner).
3. Prior work by what it covered (argument.prior-work-as-found), leading to the gap.
4. Purpose statement naming the application (argument.purpose-names-application).
5. Roadmap: one sentence per section, its role only (introduction.roadmap). A short format ({format.short} = yes)
   may skip what the abstract already says (structure.intro-no-abstract-repeat).

## Conclusion (conclusion.tense, criterion structure.conclusion-not-abstract-copy)

1. The question the paper answered, in the tense of dimension conclusion.tense.
2. Findings as mechanisms (values as dimension numbers.abstract-conclusion allows); it may share the
   abstract's wording but adds the mechanism or the recommendation the body has established.
3. Implication for the application.
4. Future work with named conditions and components.
