# Field habits: prose

- Contribution lists: numbered contribution lists in the introduction are a field norm; a separate section roadmap is
  usually absent in short papers. Related: argument.no-enumerative-scaffolding, introduction.roadmap.
- Abstract numbers: abstracts state direction only, but give dataset sizes. Related: numbers.abstract-conclusion,
  position.detail-follows-position.
- Self-reference: "this work" and "this paper" alternate; "we" is pervasive. Related: self-reference.
- Novelty: announced ("to the best of our knowledge, the first", named acronym systems) is common. Related:
  argument.no-announced-novelty.
- Models and datasets as verb subjects: "the model learns", "the dataset offers", "the system fails to detect" are
  standard field idiom. Related: wording.technical-verbs.
- Relative clauses and contrasts: ", which ..." tails, relative clauses on nouns and "while/although" contrasts are
  frequent; "However," is the dominant gap marker. Related: sentence.relative-clause,
  sentence.no-contrast-join.
- Names: coined system and dataset names are expected (proper names, not descriptive coinages). Related:
  terms.established-precise.
- Trends: stated as achievements ("has advanced considerably"). Related: wording.trend-not-obligation.
- Definitions: AMT, tablature and MIDI are defined at length because the readership is mixed (ML and music).
  Related: audience.general-practitioner.
