# Conventions: prose

Descriptive counts of field practice; each item links the related dimension or rule id.

- **Self-reference**: first person plural throughout, also by single authors; "this paper" and "this work" alternate
  freely. Passive voice appears in dataset descriptions (related: self-reference).
- **Tense**: present for the method and what tables show; past for experiments run; present perfect in conclusions
  (related: conclusion.tense).
- **Contribution bullets**: numbered lists or inline "(1) ... (2) ..." in about half the papers; dataset papers list
  artefacts (dataset, augmentation strategy, baseline results) (related: argument.no-enumerative-scaffolding).
- **Hedging**: moderate; "likely", "could be due to", "preliminary results", stated hypotheses. Novelty claims with
  "to the best of our knowledge, the first ..." (3/11) and "first large-scale ..." (related: claim.hedging,
  argument.no-announced-novelty).
- **Gap statements**: almost always data scarcity or a missing output dimension (techniques, strings, continuous
  pitch), stated as a limitation of existing datasets or systems, often opened with "However," (related:
  sentence.no-contrast-join).
- **Numbers in abstract**: mostly absent in ICASSP/ISMIR abstracts (direction only); present in dataset sizes
  ("5.9 hours") and in long numeric abstracts (related: numbers.abstract-conclusion).
- **Acronyms**: heavy (AMT, MIR, MPE, GTT, AGT); systems and datasets get a capitalised acronym name (related:
  terms.established-precise).
