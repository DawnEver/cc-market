# Field habits: prose

- Novelty: announced in most papers ("novel" in title or abstract, "main contributions are", "for the first time",
  "to the best of the authors' knowledge"). Related: argument.no-announced-novelty.
- Abstract numbers: abstracts routinely carry percentages and best-case "up to" figures; some carry operating speeds
  and power ratings. Related: numbers.abstract-conclusion, position.detail-follows-position.
- Contribution lists and previews: numbered contribution lists and multi-sentence roadmaps are common; previews
  "First, ... Then, ..." appear inside sections. Related: introduction.roadmap, structure.no-structure-preview,
  argument.no-enumerative-scaffolding.
- Relative clauses: ", which ..." tails and "that/which" relative clauses on nouns are pervasive. Related:
  sentence.relative-clause.
- Contrast: "While/Although X, Y" contrasts and ", however," mid-sentence are the default gap device. Related:
  sentence.no-contrast-join.
- Subjects: personified subjects are standard ("the paper proposes", "results reveal", "the method enables",
  "studies report"). Related: wording.technical-verbs.
- Nesting: nested "in which / so that" and long multi-clause sentences are common, especially in method
  descriptions. Related: sentence.no-nested-clause, sentence.max-words.
- Textbook models: Bertotti/Steinmetz loss formulas and critical-speed rules are restated in full with definitions.
  Related: audience.general-practitioner.
- Evaluative adjectives ("comprehensive", "significant promise", "exceptionally low", "advanced") are frequent.
  Related: wording.plain.
- Self-reference: "this work" and "this study" alternate with "this paper" within one paper. Related: self-reference.
- Synonyms: variation for the machine (motor/machine/e-machine) is common. Related: terms.one-canonical.
- Trends: obligation voice for trends ("must be considered", "require") is common in context moves. Related:
  wording.trend-not-obligation.
