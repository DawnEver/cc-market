# Conventions: prose

- Reporting verbs: present tense for this paper's content ("this paper proposes", "Fig. 5 shows");
  past tense for cited work ("[12] compared", "was shown in [14]"); "results show/indicate/reveal"
  dominant; "demonstrate" is common in abstracts for validated claims (related: wording.technical-verbs).
- Hedging: light. "slightly", "approximately", "up to [x] %" (very common in abstracts, quoting
  best-case figures); "It should be noted that" is the standard caveat opener (related: claim.hedging, wording.plain).
- Gaps: overwhelmingly "However, [prior work] [lacks/neglects/does not consider] ..."; a minority
  use "to the best of the authors' knowledge, ... has not been ..."; the strongest papers name the
  physical effect or the unfair comparison condition omitted by prior work (related: sentence.no-contrast-join,
  argument.prior-work-as-found, argument.no-announced-novelty).
- Prior-work comparison: by citation number ("[12] compares ..."), rarely by author name;
  quantitative comparison with prior results is rare; comparison is with an in-paper baseline design.
- Abstract length: 120-250 words, single paragraph; keywords line in IEEE conference format (related: abstract.word-range).
- First person: "we" rare (under 10 %); "the authors" in acknowledgements and novelty claims (related: self-reference).
- Equipment and software named freely (JMAG, Motor-CAD, ANSYS, KISSsoft) in method and acknowledgements.
